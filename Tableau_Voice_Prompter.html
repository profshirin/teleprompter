Tutorial 2Creating table joins

Transactions sits at the center of a star. Every other table describes one column of Transactions in more detail. Same shape as Northwind in Module 2.

Every transaction has exactly one site, one manager, and one customer or supplier. So those three get inner joins. But a transaction has a material or a product, never both. A purchase links to a material. A sale links to a product. So those two must be left joins, or we lose half our data.

Tutorial 3Creating calculated fields

A calculated field is a new column you build with a formula. It is the helper column from Module 1, except you write it once and it applies to all 258,602 rows.

Why do we need them? TR Subtotal holds a dollar amount for every row. Sales, returns, and purchases, all mixed together. Chart it by site and California shows about one billion dollars. That number means nothing. Split by transaction type and it becomes about 323 million in purchases and 688 million in sales. We could filter every time, but that is easy to forget. Calculated fields fix this once.

Triangle at top right of the Data pane. Create Calculated Field.

I click the little triangle at the top right of the Data pane and choose Create Calculated Field.

Name it Sales. Type the formula.

I name it Sales, with exactly that name, because later problems use these names. The formula reads, if the row is a sale and the subtotal is positive, show the subtotal.

IF [TR_TransType] = "S" AND [TR_Subtotal] > 0 THEN [TR_Subtotal] END

No ELSE needed. Tableau fills in null for every other row. We close it with END.

Point to "The calculation is valid". Click OK.

I check that it says The calculation is valid, and click OK. Sales now shows up in the Data pane.

Now the other three. Returns. If the subtotal is negative, flip it to positive.

IF [TR_Subtotal] < 0 THEN -[TR_Subtotal] END

Net sales. Every sale row, positive and negative together.

IF [TR_TransType] = "S" THEN [TR_Subtotal] END

And Purchases. Every purchase order row.

IF [TR_TransType] = "O" THEN [TR_Subtotal] END

Data Source page. Sort by TR TransType. Show the Sales column.

To check, I go to the Data Source page and sort by TR TransType. Sales shows the subtotal on positive sale rows and null on purchases and returns.

A quick question for you. Which of these four keeps returns as negative numbers? It's Net sales. Sales is revenue before returns. Returns measures returns on their own. Net sales is revenue after returns. Which one you use depends on the business question.

Tutorial 4Converting between measures and dimensions

Dimensions are things you group by, like names, codes, and dates. Measures are numbers you add up or average. In newer Tableau there is only a thin gray line between them. Dimensions above, measures below.

Tableau guesses wrong a lot. Any column of numbers comes in as a measure, even an ID code. Summing a customer ID makes no sense, so we fix these by hand.

Scan the fields below the gray line.

I go through every field below the line and ask, would I ever sum or average this?

Right click each ID field, Convert to Dimension.

These are ID codes, so they become dimensions. TR TransNumber, TR Manager, TR EntityID, TR Item. Emp EmployeeID, Ent EntityID, Ent Zip. Mat Material and Prod Product. I right click each one and choose Convert to Dimension, or just drag it above the line.

Real numbers stay as measures. Quantity, unit price, subtotal, tax, and our four calculated fields.

Right click Emp Order Approval Authority, Hide. Same for Ent RelationshipManager.

Two fields we will not use. Emp Order Approval Authority and Ent RelationshipManager. I right click each and choose Hide. It keeps the Data pane short.

Last, I glance at the dimensions to see if anything should be a measure. In this data, nothing should.

Why does this matter? Drag TR Item to Rows while it is a measure and Tableau adds up item codes. As a dimension, it groups by item. Same field, totally different chart.

Tutorial 9bReference lines, bands, and distributions

Same chart. Right click y axis. Add Reference Line. Point to the four types.

Same chart. I right click the y axis and choose Add Reference Line. Up top there are four types. Line, Band, Distribution, and Box Plot.

Line. Value Average.

Line first. I set the value to Average. You could also use Total, Sum, Minimum, Maximum, Median, or a constant.

Scope matters once you add another dimension like site to Rows. Entire Table draws one line for all the data. Per Pane gives each site its own. Per Cell gives one in each cell.

For the label, choose None, Computation, Value, or Custom to type your own text.

Add Reference Line. Band. Minimum to Maximum.

Band. I set a lower and an upper value, for example Minimum to Maximum. In formatting you can add lines at the edges. If you check Show recalculated band, it updates when you select points.

Distribution. Percentiles 5 and 95. Then Standard Deviation, factor 2.

Distribution. Here the band comes from a calculation. Percentiles 5 and 95 shade the middle 90 percent of the data. Or I choose Standard Deviation with a factor of 2, so the band runs from two below the average to two above.

That band turns your chart into a control chart. Points outside it may signal something abnormal. One catch. If the data trends upward, later points drift above the band without anything being wrong. You can fix that with a Percent Difference table calculation, or by putting year on Rows so each year gets its own band, Per Pane.

Tutorial 10Level of detail expressions

Level of detail expressions, or LOD for short. Let's build the chart first.

New sheet. TR SiteCode, Emp LastName, TR UnitPrice onto Rows. Unit price to Average.

New sheet. TR SiteCode, Emp LastName, and TR UnitPrice onto Rows. I set unit price to Average.

TR TransDate on Columns, lower Month.

TR TransDate on Columns, lower Month.

TR TransType onto Filters. Keep only O.

TR TransType onto Filters, and I keep only O. Now we see each employee's average monthly unit price for purchases, grouped by site.

Add Reference Line, Average, Per Pane. Then switch to Entire Table.

I add a reference line, Average, Per Pane. Each employee gets their own average, like 4.225 or 4.724. Switch to Entire Table and everyone shows the company average, about 3.347.

But what if I want each employee compared with their own site's average? A reference line can't do that. That's where LOD comes in.

An LOD expression groups the data by fields you choose, even fields not in the view. You write it as a calculated field inside curly brackets. There are three types. EXCLUDE, INCLUDE, and FIXED. One detail people miss. The grouping and the aggregate are separated by a colon, not a semicolon.

Try it now. Add each LOD to the chart

Create Calculated Field. Type the EXCLUDE expression.

{EXCLUDE [Emp_LastName] : AVG([TR_UnitPrice])}

EXCLUDE first. The view groups by site, employee, and month. EXCLUDE drops employee, so we get the site average for each month.

Drag onto Rows. If ATTR, Measure, Average. Dual Axis, Line, Synchronize Axis, hide right header.

I drag it onto Rows. If it shows as ATTR, I right click, Measure, Average. Then Dual Axis, set it to Line, Synchronize Axis, and hide the right header.

Now every employee in a site shows the same second line, the site's average. In California it moves between about 3.22 and 3.78. Florida has its own line, about 3.16 to 3.89.

Duplicate the sheet. Swap in INCLUDE. Repeat dual axis steps.

{INCLUDE [TR_Item] : AVG([TR_UnitPrice])}

INCLUDE. I duplicate the sheet, swap in this formula, and repeat the dual axis steps. INCLUDE adds item to the grouping. It averages each employee's price per item, and then the view averages those.

Look at Baro in California, June 2014. He bought three items, at average prices of 2.40, 6.08, and 1.77. The INCLUDE line shows 3.42, the average of those three. His real average that month is 3.57, because it counts every transaction. They only match if he bought each item the same number of times. That is the average of averages trap.

Duplicate again. Use the FIXED expression.

{FIXED [TR_SiteCode], [TR_TransType] : AVG([TR_UnitPrice])}

FIXED. I duplicate once more and use this. FIXED ignores the view and sets its own grouping. The lines are flat over time now, and a little different by site. About 3.518 for California and 3.500 for Illinois.

Why put TR TransType in there? Because FIXED ignores the view, filters included. Without it, sales and purchases would be averaged together. With it, they stay separate, and the filter keeps the purchase groups.

Create calculated field Transaction Date Month. Add it to the FIXED grouping.

MONTH([TR_TransDate])

Last one. I create a field named Transaction Date Month with MONTH of TR TransDate, and add it to the FIXED grouping. Now the site averages change from month to month.

Tutorial 3Creating calculated fields

Okay, Tutorial 3, calculated fields.

A calculated field is just a new column you make with a formula. Remember the helper column in Excel, where you typed an IF in the first cell and dragged it down? Same idea. The nice part is, in Tableau you write it once and it works on every row.

So why do we need them? Here's the thing. Our dollar column, TR Subtotal, has every kind of transaction mixed together. Sales, returns, and purchases, all in one column. So if you just add it up, you get a total that mixes money coming in with money going out. It's like adding your paycheck and your grocery bill together and calling it income. The number looks big, but it doesn't mean anything.

You could add a filter every single time to keep just the sales. But one day you'll forget, and your chart will be wrong without you knowing. A calculated field fixes this once, and then you never have to think about it again.

IF syntax

In the notes you'll see a box with the IF syntax. It's very close to Excel. IF, then your condition, THEN, your result, and END to close it.

The four fields and what they mean

Then there's a box called The four fields and what they mean. We'll build four. Sales, which is sales before returns. Returns. Net sales, which is sales after returns. And Purchases.

Tutorial 3, creating calculated fields
